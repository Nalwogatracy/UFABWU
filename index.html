Union Background & Foundation Narrative

LUN 38 · Registered 29th December 2006

The Uganda Farm and Agro-Based Workers’ Union (UFABWU) was officially established and registered on 29th December 2006 as Labour Union No. 38 (LUN 38) under the provisions of the Labour Unions Act No. 7 of 2006 and the Constitution of the Republic of Uganda (Articles 29 and 40(3)).

UFABWU was founded to address historical vulnerabilities, low wages, lack of formal representation, and unsafe working conditions faced by agricultural workers across Uganda. The union acts as a recognized legal umbrella body uniting workers across both the Public Sector (Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Industry & Fisheries - MAAIF and its specialized agencies) and the Private Sector (tea, sugar, coffee plantations, livestock, poultry, aquaculture, and agro-processing factories).

Key Historical Milestones

2006
Official Union Registration (LUN 38)

Registered on December 29, 2006 under the Labour Unions Act No. 7 of 2006 as a national labour union representing all agricultural workers in Uganda.

2008
First Major Recognition Agreements

Signed historic Recognition Agreements with leading commercial sugar and tea estates, establishing mandatory shop steward committees.

2012
National & Global Affiliations

Affiliated with COFTU (Central Organisation of Free Trade Unions) and global federations: PSI, ILO, IUF, and UISTAACT (France).

2017
Expansion to 50+ Branches & 5 Regions

Established 5 Regional Executive Councils (Central, Eastern, Northern, Western, West Nile) serving over 50 enterprise branch committees.

2026
Digital Transformation & Enterprise Growth

Empowering over 10,000 active agricultural members with digital member ID badges, confidential dispute intake, and collective bargaining coverage.

Our Vision

A just, safe, and prosperous working environment leading to increased agricultural productivity and dignified livelihoods for all farm workers.

Our Mission

Provision of a recognized, legal, and united forum for negotiations, representation, and voicing of workers' rights across public and private agricultural sectors.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn about union dues, legal protections, member eligibility, and employer relations.

Who is eligible to join UFABWU?

All workers employed in the agricultural sector in Uganda — including public service (MAAIF), private commercial crop plantations, livestock farms, poultry, fish farms, and agro-processing factories — are eligible to join.

How are union dues deducted and calculated?

Union dues are calculated as a small statutory percentage (approx 2%) of a member's basic monthly wage and are deducted directly via check-off agreement or paid via branch steward accounts. Use our interactive Dues Calculator tool for exact figures.

What legal protection does UFABWU provide?

UFABWU provides free legal aid representation in the Industrial Court, handles wrongful termination disputes, negotiates compensation for occupational injuries, and enforces Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs).
